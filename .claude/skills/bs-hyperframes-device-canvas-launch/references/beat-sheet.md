# Beat sheet

Durations are the reference cut at 1920x1080 / 30fps, total 18.0s.

| Beat | Seconds | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 · arrival | 0.0–4.0 | The device rises into the studio carrying the screen; the shadow settles under it |
| 2 · scroll | 4.0–7.5 | An oversized cursor arrives and swipes; the page scrolls; the device turns |
| 3 · the action | 7.5–11.5 | Into the interface. One line typed, one press, one answer |
| 4 · the claim | 11.5–14.0 | One line, then a colour split |
| 5 · the reveal | 14.0–18.0 | The CTA, then the reveal to the lockup |

## The copy slots

| Slot | Count | Budget | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `typed` | 1 | ≤ 40 chars | Beat 3. What gets typed. It has to be something the product accepts |
| `answer` | 1 | ≤ 44 chars | Beat 3. What the product actually replies |
| `claim` | 1 | ≤ 30 chars | Beat 4. One line. Not a feature |
| `cta` | 2 | ≤ 16 chars each | Beat 5. Two short lines |
| `wordmark` | 1 | ≤ 12 chars | Beat 5 |

## Beat 1 — arrival

The device comes up from below frame, decelerating, and settles with a small overshoot. The
shadow arrives with it but lags by two or three frames, which is what sells the weight.

The studio is dark and nearly empty: one soft pool of light behind the device, and nothing
else. Resist adding furniture. Everything in this frame that is not the device is competing
with the screen.

## Beat 2 — scroll

The cursor enters on a curve, presses once, and swipes. The page scrolls from 0 to about 0.7
of its height, decelerating — a scroll that reaches the bottom reads as a page ending rather
than a page continuing.

The device turns slightly during the scroll, no more than about 20°, and returns. The turn is
what proves it is an object.

## Beat 3 — the action

The film goes inside. One line is typed, one control is pressed, and one answer arrives.

**One.** Not "type, send, then also open the menu". Two actions turn a launch into a tutorial,
and a tutorial is longer, slower, and for someone else.

The answer should be the product's real behaviour, at its real speed if that is under two
seconds and compressed honestly if it is not — and the handoff should say which.

## Beat 4 — the claim

One line, held. Then a colour split: the frame's red, green and blue channels separate by a
few pixels for about a fifth of a second and re-converge.

Two rules for the split. It happens **once**, and it happens on the claim rather than
between beats — a split used as a transition is a wipe with extra steps.

## Beat 5 — the reveal

The CTA is two short lines on a black frame. Then a shape opens from the centre and consumes
the frame, revealing the lockup on a light ground.

The inversion matters: the whole film has been dark, so the lockup landing on light is the
only time the frame changes state. Ending dark on dark spends that for nothing.

## Retiming

Beat 2 is the one that can be shortened. Beat 3 cannot: without a completed action the film
is a device on a table, and the device was never the point.
